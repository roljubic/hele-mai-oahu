O'ahu Grocery & Food Shopping Guide for New Residents: Where to Shop and What to Budget

By Hele Mai O'ahu Relocation Team Updated: May 2026 12 min read

One of the first things new O'ahu residents discover is that food costs more here. That's not a surprise — Hawaii is a chain of islands in the middle of the Pacific, and virtually everything you buy arrived on a ship or a plane. But knowing where to shop, what to buy locally versus imported, and how to build a realistic grocery budget can make a significant difference in your monthly expenses.

This guide covers every major grocery option on O'ahu — from Costco runs to farmers markets to specialty Asian grocery stores — along with honest monthly budget ranges for individuals, couples, and families.

The Real Cost of Food on O'ahu

Let's start with the honest number: groceries on O'ahu run approximately 30–50% higher than the U.S. mainland average. The USDA's food cost data typically doesn't capture Hawaii's reality — so don't use it for planning.

Some specific comparisons to give you a feel:

  • Gallon of milk: $5.50–$7.00 (mainland average ~$3.50)
  • Dozen eggs: $5.00–$8.00 depending on store and type
  • Loaf of bread: $4.50–$7.00
  • Ground beef (1 lb): $7.00–$12.00
  • Boneless chicken breast (1 lb): $5.50–$9.00
  • Avocados: $1.50–$3.00 each (imported — local ones are seasonal)
  • Bananas: Often cheaper than the mainland — local farms supply year-round
  • Papayas: $1.00–$2.00 — local and plentiful

The good news: locally grown produce (papayas, bananas, taro, certain greens, fresh fish) can actually be less expensive than on the mainland when purchased at farmers markets or local fish markets. The key is knowing what grows here versus what has to be shipped in.

Major Grocery Chains on O'ahu

Foodland

Foodland is O'ahu's homegrown grocery chain — locally owned and operated since 1948. With over a dozen locations across the island (Kapolei, Pearl City, Kaimuki, Kailua, Kaneohe, and more), it's usually the most conveniently located store for most neighborhoods.

Prices are mid-range. The Maika'i card (free loyalty card) gives regular discounts and is worth getting immediately. Foodland does a good job stocking local products — Kahuku shrimp, Hamakua mushrooms, local poi — that you won't find at mainland chains.

Best for: Everyday shopping, local products, convenient locations
Price level: $$

Times Supermarket

Times is the value option among the mainstream chains. Prices are generally lower than Safeway or Foodland, and the stores carry a solid mix of local and imported products. Popular locations include Moiliili, Kalihi, and Pearl City.

The produce section skews toward Asian vegetables, reflecting Hawaii's demographic mix — you'll find bitter melon, daikon, long beans, and napa cabbage alongside standard American produce.

Best for: Budget-conscious everyday shopping
Price level: $–$$

Safeway

The mainland chain has a strong presence on O'ahu with multiple locations (Beretania, Kapahulu, Kailua, Mililani, Hawaii Kai). Safeway prices are higher than Times or Foodland, but the stores are well-stocked and familiar for mainland transplants. The Club Card is free and worth using.

Best for: Familiar brands, well-organized layouts, large prepared foods sections
Price level: $$–$$$

Whole Foods

One location in Kahala. Well-stocked with organic and specialty items, excellent prepared food bar, good local produce section. Expensive — but if you're shopping for organic or specialty dietary needs, it's your best option.

Best for: Organic, specialty diets, premium prepared foods
Price level: $$$–$$$$

Trader Joe's

One location in Kakaako (Ward area). Extremely popular — lines can be long on weekends. TJ's is worth visiting for affordable wine, specialty snacks, and unique pantry items not available elsewhere on the island. They don't carry a lot of local Hawaiian products, but for mainland staples at reasonable prices, it's a good supplement to your main grocery store.

Best for: Wine, specialty pantry items, affordable snacks
Price level: $$

Target & Walmart

Both have grocery sections that are useful for pantry staples, cleaning supplies, and household goods. Multiple Walmart locations (Kalihi, Pearl City, Kapolei) carry a full grocery section. Not a replacement for a real grocery store but handy for bulk staples at lower prices.

Best for: Household staples, pantry items
Price level: $–$$

Asian & Specialty Markets

O'ahu's demographic is roughly 35–40% Asian, and the island's Asian grocery scene reflects that. These stores are essential for any resident who cooks Asian food — or who simply wants more variety at better prices for certain items.

Don Quijote (Donki)

Japanese-owned and 24 hours at the Kalihi/Nimitz location, Don Quijote is a full-service grocery with a strong Asian foods section, competitive prices, and an excellent prepared food and hot deli counter. A second location is in Waipahu. The bulk bin and discount produce sections offer real savings.

Best for: Japanese groceries, bulk staples, late-night shopping, prepared foods
Price level: $–$$

Mitsuwa Marketplace

Located in the Ala Moana area, Mitsuwa is the Japanese specialty grocery on the island. If you want specific Japanese brands — particular soy sauces, specialty ramen, mochi, high-grade wagyu — this is where you go. Prices are higher than Don Quijote but the selection is unmatched for Japanese products.

Best for: Premium Japanese groceries, specialty ingredients
Price level: $$$

H Mart

H Mart's Honolulu location serves the Korean and pan-Asian community with an enormous selection of Korean, Chinese, Japanese, and Southeast Asian ingredients. Great banchan selection, fresh tofu, Korean BBQ meats, and affordable produce. One of the better produce deals on the island for Asian vegetables.

Best for: Korean groceries, Asian produce, bulk kimchi and banchan
Price level: $–$$

Palama Supermarket (Kalihi)

A neighborhood fixture in Kalihi, Palama is a Filipino-focused market with good prices on fresh fish, tropical produce, and specialty Filipino ingredients. Local and authentic — worth the trip if you're in the area.

Marukai (Now Part of Don Quijote)

Several former Marukai locations are now Don Quijote stores. The Pearl City location is popular with West O'ahu residents.

Costco: The Island Staple

Ask any longtime O'ahu resident about their grocery strategy and Costco will come up within the first 30 seconds. There are two locations: Iwilei (near downtown Honolulu) and Salt Lake (near Tripler/Halawa). Both are consistently busy.

For island living, Costco's value proposition is stronger than anywhere on the mainland. Bulk buying reduces per-unit cost significantly, and the shipping economics that inflate grocery prices don't disappear at Costco — but they're spread across larger quantities.

What to buy at Costco on O'ahu:

  • Meat and seafood — among the best prices on the island for quality cuts; the salmon and shrimp are popular
  • Dairy — milk, butter, cheese, eggs at dramatically lower prices than grocery chains
  • Paper goods and cleaning supplies — non-negotiable savings
  • Alcohol — Kirkland wine and spirits are significantly cheaper than grocery stores
  • Fresh produce — large quantities; best if you have a family or cook frequently
  • Pantry staples — olive oil, pasta, rice, canned goods, nuts
  • Gas — Costco gas is consistently 20–40 cents/gallon cheaper than most stations on O'ahu

The membership fee ($65/year basic) pays for itself quickly on the island. Most O'ahu families recoup it in 2–3 Costco runs.

Tip: Go early on weekday mornings to avoid the infamous weekend crowds. The Iwilei location has parking challenges — arrive before 10 AM or after 2 PM on weekdays.

Farmers Markets on O'ahu

O'ahu has a strong farmers market culture, and for produce, eggs, honey, and local specialty items, markets are often cheaper and always fresher than grocery stores. Here are the main ones:

KCC Farmers Market (Diamond Head)

When: Saturdays, 7:30–11:00 AM
Where: Kapiolani Community College, Diamond Head Road
What: The largest and most popular market on the island. Excellent local produce, fresh fish, baked goods, prepared foods, and artisan products. Gets crowded after 9 AM — arrive early for the best selection. Ample parking in the KCC lot but fills up fast.

Kailua Town Farmers Market

When: Sundays, 8:30 AM–12:00 PM
Where: Kailua Town Center parking area
What: More intimate than KCC with a strong community feel. Good produce, local honey, eggs, and food trucks. A weekend ritual for Kailua and Windward Side residents.

Mililani Farmers Market

When: Sundays, 8:00 AM–12:00 PM
Where: Mililani High School
What: Popular with Central and North Shore residents. Good variety, less crowded than KCC.

Honolulu Farmers Market (Blaisdell)

When: Wednesdays, 4:00–7:00 PM
Where: Neal S. Blaisdell Center
What: Great for weekday shopping. Local vendors, prepared foods, produce, and specialty items. Good option if you can't make weekend markets.

Kakaako Farmers Market

When: Saturdays, 8:00 AM–12:00 PM
Where: Blaisdell Exhibition Hall (Ward area)
What: Compact but well-curated. Popular with the urban Honolulu crowd. Good coffee, specialty foods, and local produce.

What to buy at O'ahu farmers markets:

  • Papayas, bananas, starfruit, lilikoi (passionfruit)
  • Local greens, sweet potato, taro
  • Farm-fresh eggs (often $6–8/dozen but from local farms)
  • Local honey (Manuka and wildflower varieties)
  • Fresh fish — particularly on O'ahu's North Shore
  • Poi (taro paste) — a Hawaiian staple, often freshly made

Shopping by Neighborhood

Where you live on O'ahu shapes your grocery options significantly. Here's a quick breakdown:

Honolulu / East O'ahu (Kahala, Kaimuki, Hawaii Kai)

Excellent options: Whole Foods in Kahala, multiple Foodland and Safeway locations, KCC Farmers Market on Saturdays. Hawaii Kai has a Times Supermarket and Safeway. Well-served overall.

Urban Honolulu (Downtown, Kakaako, Ala Moana)

Trader Joe's in Kakaako, Mitsuwa near Ala Moana, H Mart in the area, multiple grocery options. Don Quijote in Kalihi is a quick drive. Well-served for variety.

Kailua / Windward Side

Foodland and Safeway in Kailua, Times in Kaneohe. Kailua Farmers Market on Sundays. Closest Costco is Iwilei (about 25–30 min over the Pali). Some residents make weekly Costco runs part of their routine.

North Shore (Haleiwa)

The most limited grocery access. Foodland Haleiwa is the main option. Many North Shore residents do a major weekly shop in Town (Honolulu) and supplement locally. Haleiwa has good local fruit stands and fish markets along the highway.

West O'ahu (Kapolei, Ewa Beach, Ko Olina)

Fast-growing area with increasingly good options: Costco Salt Lake is accessible, multiple Walmart and Target locations in Kapolei, Foodland and Times. The development of Kapolei has brought more retail competition and pricing has improved.

Central O'ahu (Mililani, Aiea, Pearl City)

Well-served. Multiple Foodland, Times, and Safeway locations. Pearl City Don Quijote for Asian groceries. Costco Salt Lake is close. Good value for central residents.

Monthly Food Budget Guide for O'ahu Residents

These are realistic estimates for home cooking — not dining out — using a mix of Costco, local chains, and occasional farmers market visits:

Household Budget Range / Month Notes
Single adult $500–$750 Cooking most meals at home; Costco membership pays off at 2+ people
Couple $800–$1,200 Costco helps significantly; mix of home cooking and eating out
Family of 4 $1,200–$1,800 Costco essential; budget more if kids are picky eaters or you eat organic
Family of 4 (premium) $1,800–$2,500+ Whole Foods + organic + specialty + frequent dining out

Note: These figures are for groceries only. Dining out on O'ahu is also more expensive than the mainland — expect $15–$25/person for a casual lunch and $35–$80+/person for dinner at a mid-range restaurant.

Money-Saving Tips for O'ahu Residents

1. Get a Costco Membership Immediately

This single step saves most families $100–$200/month on groceries alone. Add in the gas savings and it becomes the best $65/year you'll spend on O'ahu.

2. Shop Loyalty Programs at Every Chain

Foodland's Maika'i card, Safeway's Club Card, and Don Quijote's discount card are all free. Use all of them. The savings on weekly specials add up meaningfully over time.

3. Buy Local Seasonal Produce

Imported fruit and vegetables pay a double shipping premium. Papayas, bananas, certain greens, and root vegetables grown locally are often cheaper and always fresher. Learn the local seasons.

4. Fish Markets Over Grocery Stores for Seafood

Honolulu's Chinatown has excellent fish markets (Oahu Market, Young's Fish Market) with significantly lower prices than grocery chains for fresh fish. The Honolulu Fish Auction (primarily wholesale) also has retail access. Living on a Pacific island should mean access to fresh fish at reasonable prices — and it does if you know where to shop.

5. Reduce the "Import Premium" Items

Certain items carry enormous shipping premiums for no real quality benefit — bottled water, soda, and bulky non-perishables especially. Buy a good water filter (Brita or a whole-house unit) and stop buying bottled water. Skip the six-pack of soda at $10 and make lemonade with local citrus.

6. Join a CSA or Local Farm Box

Several O'ahu farms offer CSA (Community Supported Agriculture) boxes — weekly or biweekly deliveries of locally grown produce at flat rates. MA'O Organic Farms (Leeward), Ho Farms, and Kahumana Organic Farm are popular options. Quality is excellent and prices are fair for what you get.

7. Freeze What You Can

Meat, fish, and bread all freeze well. Buy in bulk at Costco, portion and freeze, and you cut significant per-meal cost. A chest freezer (under $200) is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade for O'ahu families.

8. Use the "Local vs. Imported" Mental Filter

Before you grab something at the grocery store, quickly ask: does this grow in Hawaii? If yes, it's probably cheaper and fresher at a farmers market. If no, you're paying the shipping premium — which is fine for some things, but worth being intentional about.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much more expensive is food in Hawaii compared to the mainland?

Groceries on O'ahu typically run 30–50% higher than the U.S. mainland average. A family of four should budget $1,200–$1,800/month for groceries depending on their shopping habits. Cooking at home and shopping at Costco or local markets can significantly reduce that number.

What is the cheapest grocery store on O'ahu?

Costco (Iwilei and Salt Lake) offers the lowest per-unit prices for bulk staples. For everyday groceries, Times Supermarket, Foodland, and Don Quijote are more affordable than Whole Foods or Safeway. Farmers markets are great for produce at competitive prices.

Are there Asian grocery stores on O'ahu?

Yes. O'ahu has excellent Asian grocery options including Mitsuwa Marketplace (Japanese), H Mart (Korean and pan-Asian), Don Quijote (Japanese-owned with a wide Asian selection), and numerous smaller Chinese, Filipino, and Korean specialty shops throughout Honolulu and Kalihi.

Is Whole Foods or Trader Joe's on O'ahu?

Yes to both. There is one Whole Foods in Kahala and a Trader Joe's in Kakaako. Trader Joe's is well-stocked and popular with residents for specialty items and affordable wine. Whole Foods carries premium local produce and prepared foods.

When are the best farmers markets on O'ahu?

The KCC Farmers Market (Diamond Head) runs Saturdays 7:30–11 AM and is the most popular. Kailua Town Farmers Market is Sundays 8:30 AM–12 PM. The Mililani Farmers Market runs Sundays. There are also weekday markets at various locations around the island.
